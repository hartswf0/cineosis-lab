CAMERA A

The phone screen is the light source. The can is a curved mirror. The camera reads the screen's code in the can and works out where the can is over the screen, in millimetres.

The simulation computes a camera image by reflecting view rays off a cylinder onto a coded screen, adds ambient light, noise and colour error, and decodes from those pixels.

WHAT LIMITS THIS

  • A region, not a point. The can is a convex mirror. One camera pixel on the can collects light from a patch of screen, and the patch is long near the can's left and right edges. The code is a linear ramp so that the average of a patch decodes to the centre of the patch. At the screen edge part of the patch is off the screen and the reading is pulled inward.
  • Most of the can does not show the screen. A view ray that meets the can at height z leaves it downward and lands z / tan(camera elevation) further along. Only cells whose ray lands on the screen carry code. With the can lifted, or the camera low, few cells are lit and the answer is weak along the line to the camera. The amber box is the fit's estimate of that.
  • Room light. The can also reflects the table and scatters room light. The 4-frame code shows black, white, x-ramp, y-ramp and uses (ramp − black) / (white − black) per cell, which removes room light and the can's own colour if the can and the light hold still for the four frames. The 1-frame colour code uses room light measured at calibration, which is wrong after the can moves.
  • Colour and tone. The camera's red, green and blue each respond to all three screen colours. CALIBRATE flashes black, white, red, green, blue and three greys, reads them in the can, and builds a 3x3 unmixing matrix and a tone curve. Camera auto-exposure and auto white balance change between frames and break this; the page asks the camera to lock them and says if it could not.
  • Printed cans. Ink scatters screen light from the whole screen into every cell, which pulls the reading toward the screen centre. A bare aluminium can works best. The bottom 10 mm of a real can is tapered and that taper is not in the model.
  • The box. The sampling box must span the can's full width, because the horizontal place of a cell in the box is what tells the fit which way that part of the can faces.
  • The camera has to look down on the can. If the camera is level with or below the sampled band, the reflected rays go upward and never reach the phone.
  • The simulated numbers are from simulated pixels. No number on this page comes from real hardware until a real camera and phone are connected.
