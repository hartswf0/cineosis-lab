The Operative Archive · Cineosis Lab

Line, circle, dot.

Three elements every era of image-making has drawn. A line is time and the thread. A circle is the reel, the lens, the eye, the O. A dot is where attention rests: the bead, the reader. The whole identity, and Bolas with it, is made of nothing else.

Why the small eyes went

They were a spider's eight eyesTrue to the animal, but at any size below a hand they read as dots of dirt around a head, and they made the mark a cartoon.
One dot does moreA single dot inside the O can look anywhere, be anywhere on the line, be solid, hatched or empty. Everything the six eyes did, it does by position.
The legs are behaviourThe character keeps its legs and its face for when it performs. The mark never needs them: an O hanging on a line already is a spider, a reel, a pendulum, a plumb bob, an eye.

The mark

One line, three states

The same line, carrying the same shots as beads. Wound, it is the reel: the archive in storage. Pulled straight, it is the timeline: a film playing. Woven, it is the web: what the shots have to do with one another. The O travels it as the reader.

Reelthe archive wound on itself: everything kept, in the order it was made
Lineone film unwound: time, a playhead, shots in sequence
Webthe relations: the same shot in many films, readings that agree, questions that join
now: reelthe dot: where the reader is

As timelines

The same three elements are the interface for any film in the archive. Each shot is a bead; its dot says what is known about it; the O is the reader, wherever it is.

Any era

An idea is eternal if it survives every way of drawing. The same mark, made as each period would have made it.

The forty-five signs, in three elements

Every sign the lab reads for, rebuilt from only a line, a circle and dots. The family decides what the line does; the sign decides where the dot goes.
