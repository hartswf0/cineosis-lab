Bolas Pixel

Each film and sign as one pixel icon that moves: designed on a 32-pixel grid, so it holds up as a favicon and can be read from across a room. Bolas has four limbs now, two arms and two legs. Either its body makes the symbol, or it spins the symbol out of its web while you watch.

Body symbolsThe body and the arms form the sign: scissors, scales, a reel, a clock, a boat.
Web symbolsBolas spins the sign strand by strand: a question mark, an eye, a tree, a stave, a hexagon. A web is thread, so it is red.
Four limbsEight legs read as noise at this size. Two arms that do things and two legs that stand.
One ideaEach icon has a single silhouette you could draw from memory after seeing it once.

The signs

A sign is an O on its thread, holding its symbol in its family's colour, with the head below. Around it, the family's web: sight lines, ripples, a dash, a mirror, a net, an orbit, a break, lines of text.

Every icon is drawn pixel by pixel in the page, twenty frames at ten frames a second. The 16-pixel version keeps a pixel wherever any of the four it replaces was drawn, so thin lines survive. Sign symbols are shown in capitals in the pixel font.
