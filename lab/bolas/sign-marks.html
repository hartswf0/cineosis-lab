Cineosis Sign Marks

One master mark, then three ideas for each of the 45 signs the lab reads for, after Deleuze's cinema books as tabulated by David Deamer. Every mark is built from the same few parts, so the 45 read as one family and any of them can sit on a favicon, a card or a film slate.

The parts

Framethe square: a shot, an interval, the evidence.
Red linethe operation: the cut, the playhead, a force acting. One red operation per mark; it may repeat, as a mark does.
Cellsolid is documented, hatched is inferred, empty is unknown.
Circlea centre: a face, a voice, a seed, a past.
Goldused once, in the signs where a person's own voice or face carries the sign.
Family codein the letter marks, the red stroke's angle says the family, so the symbol alone tells you which regime you are in.

The master mark

The colour

One colour carries the brand: Cue Red. It already means the operation in every film and room, the playhead and the cut, so it stays rare: one red operation in any view. Everything else is archive black and paper. Gold and lime keep the two meanings they have earned, and appear only when that meaning is there.

The avatar: Cue

Cue is a frame that walks: a square shot with one eye, on two legs, and one leg is red, exactly as the red stroke completes the A in OA. It is named for the cue marks a projectionist watches for in the corner of a film, which were operative images long before machines read films.

Cue shows up wherever the system has something to say about itself: loading, empty results, a clip that will not play, the end card, the favicon. Its body is the certainty code: solid when something is documented, hatched when it is inferred, an outline when it is unknown. Cue never pretends to know more than it does.

Questions are the lab's own, one per sign. Marks are proposals for review, not final drawings.
