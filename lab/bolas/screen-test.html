Bolas Screen Test

Before an actor is cast they are filmed counting down, turning, reacting. This is Bolas's: inside the O of the archive, which is also the countdown circle at the head of every reel. Then the acting: one face rig, and three shots built the way hand-drawn animation is, from keys, breakdowns and in-betweens.

The rules of the details

Red is only the threadThe line Bolas hangs from, throws, cuts with. Never a leg, never a stripe, never part of a letter. Wherever red appears, an operation is happening.
The O is a circle with a jobThe countdown, the lens, the reel, the abdomen. It is drawn as a ring, never filled, so something can always happen inside it.
The A is all inkIts two legs stand; its crossbar is the ground line Bolas can stand on.
Gold is a beadA caught voice or a person's reading, always a small round bead on the thread, never a fill.
The eyes do the actingTwo big eyes with lids; six small eyes that work as brows and light gold when it reads.
Small is drawn, not shrunkAt sixteen pixels every symbol is redrawn on the grid; the thread stays one pixel and stays red.

The countdown, ten to one

The O is the leader's circle. Each count is one beat of the screen test: Bolas drops into the circle and gives a different reading of the same frame, until one, and the cut.

The expression sheet

A close-up: one head, twelve states. Lids, pupils, the six small eyes used as brows, the fangs, and the front pair of legs used as arms. Every state below is a key the animation can travel between.

Three acting shots

Each shot is built from numbered keys, with breakdowns that say how the motion happens and in-betweens spaced for weight. The strip under each shot is its onion skin: every drawing at once.

The small symbols

The details game: every symbol of the Operative Archive redrawn on a sixteen-pixel grid, shown at four times and at actual size.
