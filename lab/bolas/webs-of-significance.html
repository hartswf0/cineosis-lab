Webs of Significance

“man is an animal suspended in webs of significance he himself has spun”
CLIFFORD GEERTZ, AFTER MAX WEBER · THE INTERPRETATION OF CULTURES, 1973

No diagram on this page was drawn first and filled in afterwards. Every thread here is something the lab actually did: a question it asked, a shot the archive returned, a shot a take chose, a source two takes both reached for. Spun from the bottom up, the archive's web looks like this.

Geertz also describes culture as a set of control mechanisms rather than a pile of customs: plans, recipes, rules, instructions, the things engineers call programs (paraphrase). The lab has those too. Four short recipes govern the four takes, and the same line run through four recipes comes out as four different films.

One line, and everything spun around it

Chapter · patch

The line is at the hub. The lab asked the archive four questions about it, one of each kind. The archive answered each with shots, ranked by score. Red threads run from the shots a take actually used to the take that used them. Hover any shot for its title, year and score.

The recipes

Four programs, written down before any shot was chosen. They are the whole difference between the four films.

The loom

Twelve lines from across the film, left to right, and the shot each recipe chose for them. Each take is one red thread running through its beads. Where a thread thickens, the take stayed with the same source film from one line to the next.

The sieve

What the questions caught across the whole film, and how little of it was kept.

Threads between takes

The source films that more than one take reached for. Each take sits at a corner; each shared film hangs between the takes that used it, pulled toward the one that used it most. The thread's weight is how many times.

Data: the Lesson 7 worked example (its questions, hits, scores and uses), the four takes of the WYGWYL suite (169 patches each, with each choice's recorded reason), the recipe sentences as written for each take, and the question sieve totals.
