Bolas, as a system

One mark that is a spider, two letters, a playhead and a measurement at once, drawn from the lab's most reused piece of film, and able to carry any production, any material and any scale. And because it can be drawn from data, the mark itself can be an operative image: each film's mark reports what was done to it.

The reduction

From the cartoon to the emblem in four steps, each one a frame. Every step keeps the three things that make it Bolas: a round body, legs that spread down, and a red line from above.

The spider engineer becomes OA

Hanging from its line, a spider's silhouette is already two letters: the round abdomen is an O, the legs spreading down make an A. The red line comes from above, and, as in your OA mark, one leg of the A is red: the operation completes the letter.

Ways to read it

One mark, several readings, and none of them is the final one. Put it next to different things and it becomes a different instrument. That is the lab's own thesis, held in a single drawing.

Scales

Each size gets its own drawing, not a shrunk copy: the eyes and the stripe drop out as it gets smaller, and the line and the O stay to the last pixel.

As film

The ident is drawn the way film is: twelve frames on a strip, with sprocket holes and frame numbers, the line swinging across a shot and cutting it. Read left to right it is a flipbook; projected at speed it is the opener.

Every Cineosis mark, with Bolas

Each production keeps its own sign. Bolas does not replace them; it arrives and does that production's operation. Left: the mark as it is now (redrawn from the current favicons). Right: the same mark with Bolas at work.

Materials

The same mark in every place the lab speaks: on screen, in film, on paper, in a room.

An operative image

The mark can be drawn from a film's own data, so it is not only a logo but a reading of the film: how long the thread is, how many eyes are lit, whether a voice is caught, how the film's claims divide into documented, inferred and unknown.
