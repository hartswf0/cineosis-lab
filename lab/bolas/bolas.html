Bolas

A bolas spider hunts with one line, swung through the air. Ours drops on the red line into a shot, reads it with eight eyes, and swings: the thread sweeping across the frame is the cut. It comes from Spider Engineers (1956), the film our six films used more than any other.

K01

Why this one

It reads as a silhouetteA round body on a straight line is legible at sixteen pixels and on a wall. Nobody confuses it with anything else in the lab.
It owns the red lineThe line is not decoration any more: it is the thread Bolas hangs from, drops on, and cuts with. Wherever the playhead appears, Bolas could be at the end of it.
It wants somethingIt hunts frames. Every appearance is an action: drop, read, swing, catch, climb. That is what the lab does to a film.
It comes from the archiveNot invented decoration: the lab's most reused actor, from a 1956 educational film about spiders as builders, turned into its guide.
Eight eyes, many readingsTwo large eyes look; six small eyes light one by one when it reads, one per machine. One body, several ways of seeing, no single answer.
Its web is the tableWhen it rests it sits on a web, and the web is the grid of signs: solid, hatched and empty cells, the certainty code.
