Webs That Matter

Bateson called information “a difference which makes a difference.” Here every sign is drawn as its defining difference, and every film's web is spun from that film's own data. Nothing on this page is decoration. Each strand is a number the lab measured.

The table of signs, as one web

Forty-five signs in their places on the table. Each red thread is a confusion the lab recorded: two signs that get taken for each other. Click a sign to see it beside the three it is confused with, and the difference that tells them apart.

The films, spun from their data

Bolas spins each web, strand by strand, from what that film actually measured. The same web, drawn at 32 pixels, is the film's favicon, so the icon is the data too.

The signs, one by one

Each pictogram shows the sign's test, the thing you would look for on screen. The small strip under it is the sign's own constellation: where it sits on the table, and the three signs it reaches for by mistake.

Data: CLIP medians and BLIP captions from the machines film; search counts from Where the Archive Came From; speaking speed of 267 lines; the 1,342-candidate score distribution from Choosing; join types from the syntagma room; 111 poem line durations; the 169-shot sequences of the four takes and their overlaps; the syzygy tide; the recurring cast; chemistry trial measures; scenes per book of the Odyssey; takes per Republic episode; the sign table's differences and confusions. Source film titles are left out. The Bateson phrase is quoted from “Steps to an Ecology of Mind” (1972).
