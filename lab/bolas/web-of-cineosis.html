The five kingdoms

Kinds of web

Spiders build different webs for different prey. So does the lab. Each kind of web is one operation on the moving image, with the machines that perform it and the place you can watch it happen.

How a film gets spun

An orb weaver builds in phases: a loose proto-web, then radii, then a scaffolding spiral, then the sticky capture spiral, then a signature. A Cineosis film is built the same way. The numbers are the WYGWYL suite's.

Radar

A radar is a web with its spokes labelled. First, what each machine is actually given: its input and output, by design. No two machines make the same shape, so none of them sees the same film.

How the Cineosis take reads

The 169 shots of the Cineosis take, counted by the family of each sign they were chosen for. Time and the mental image dominate; the readable image barely appears.

Routing against affinity

For each kind of sign, how many shots the syzygy router placed (red) against what affinity alone would have found (ink), as a share of the total. Where the red reaches further, the routing found what plain similarity missed.

The entanglement

The web we have spun so far. Nine machines in the centre, the operations around them, and the films on the outside, added one at a time in the order of the curriculum. Each strand joins a film to an operation it shows, or a machine to an operation it performs.

Counts come from the lab's own data: the archive search totals, the WYGWYL suite scope, the lesson datasets, the Cineosis take's sign assignments and the syzygy routing results. Findings quoted on the web cards were measured in the machines film. A machine's radar shows its inputs and outputs by design, not a score. The spider-web building phases follow the published orb-weaver studies; the mapping to filmmaking is ours.
