1 Solid perception

Either a clear point-of-view shot (framing that stands in for a person's eyes: looking down at own hands, through a vehicle windscreen, up from a bed or through binoculars/keyhole), or a single figure held dead-centre with the world arranged around them.

2 Liquid perception

A visibly moving camera (tracking, crane, dolly, handheld follow) that passes between people or spaces within the shot, or a flowing water/boat-mounted movement where no single figure holds the centre.

3 Gaseous perception

A visibly non-human or manipulated image: time-lapse, extreme slow motion, superimposition/multiple exposure, negative, microscopy, X-ray or thermal view, or a kaleidoscopic/duplicated pattern where no subject dominates.

4 Icon

A close-up or extreme close-up in which a single face (or eyes/mouth) fills most of the frame and carries visible emotion or intense attention.

5 Dividual

A crowd, pack, flock or massed group sharing one visible reaction or motion (cheering, fleeing, marching, swarming), ideally with individual faces still legible within the mass.

6 Any-space-whatever

A shot with no human focus (or a tiny, overwhelmed figure) where the space itself dominates: fog, rain, heat haze, lens flare, deep shadow, ruin, empty interior or blank backdrop.

7 Symptom

A figure (human or animal) visibly seized by raw drive: devouring food, fighting, clawing, crawling on all fours, frenzied grabbing at money or prey.

8 Fetish

An insert/close-up of a single charged object singled out and lit or held reverently: amulet, relic, idol, ring, locket, charm, shoe, key, a raw piece of meat.

9 Originary world

A primal environment dominating the frame with menace or timelessness – dense dark forest, desert, swamp, cave/tunnel, isolated old house, fire at night – with humans small or absent.

10 Index of lack

A shot of active searching or disclosure: someone investigating (with a flashlight, magnifier, opening a door, lifting a sheet, reading a clue) or an image whose meaning is revealed within the frame.

11 Index of equivocity

A single visibly ambiguous action or composition that reads two ways: a gesture that could be tender or violent, a split/forked path, two doors, mirror or double framing, a smile over a threat.

12 Vector

One frame holds several independent people, vehicles or creatures in one shared space, each clearly moving along its own path or doing its own task, with no single centre.

13 Milieu

An establishing or extreme wide shot where the environment (town, landscape, factory, institution, classroom system) dominates and the people are small parts of it; or an explanatory diagram of a social order.

14 Binomial

Two opposed parties (people, groups, animals) visibly face or confront each other in the frame, or one side stares down the other across a clear divide.

15 Imprint

A lone figure visibly weighed down by their surroundings, or a charged personal object (toy, record, photo, animal) shown in close relation to a person, so the environment reads as pressing on them.

16 Plastic figure

A single object, creature or staged spectacle clearly standing in for something larger (metaphor/metonymy), for example fruit in place of water, animal slaughter in place of massacre, or a sudden stylised musical number.

17 Theatrical figure

A visibly staged, rehearsed, cartoon or mock version of a conflict (drill, puppet fight, stage duel, war game, animated battle) standing in for a real one.

18 Mise en abyme

An image visibly contains another image or replica of itself or its world: a screen showing a screen, mirrors facing each other, a model within the scene, a film being shown inside the film.

19 Figure of the sublime

A visible act of grandiose over-reach or zealous spectacle, oversized relative to its means or setting: a lone zealot, a doomed grand machine, a mass rally, a stunt that fails.

20 Figure of enfeeblement

A small, idle or powerless figure visibly dwarfed by a large setting, or people shown doing nothing (sitting, waiting, drifting) while the environment dominates.

21 Quotidian

An ordinary, unremarkable daily activity or domestic/work space filmed plainly and patiently (curtains, desk, pool, meal, commute) with nothing dramatic happening in frame.

22 Limit of the large form action-image

A situation built for conflict (wall, poison, masks, hunting, evacuation) whose image makes the aggressor/victim relation ambiguous or reversible; the frame raises a question instead of showing a duel.

23 Limit of the small form action-image

A shot where separate figures or actions share a space without connecting (passing, facing away, split apart), or an isolated clue/fragment that resolves nothing; no convergence or payoff visible in frame.

24 Limit of the action-image (extreme limit)

An indeterminate problem-space or cipher object: an empty/any-space-whatever setting, a maze, an imprint or absence standing for an answer, or a labour/social milieu shown as an open question rather than a plot situation.

25 Rich dream

A single shot visibly marked as dream by optical means: superimposition/double exposure, dissolve, distortion, trick-film impossibility, or a sleeper with dream imagery overlaid; plain realist shots do not count.

26 Restrained dream

An unmarked, realist shot (no superimposition or dissolve) of an everyday setting or object that reads as uncanny: a drowsing or dazed figure, dim half-light, an object isolated and oddly emphasised.

27 Movement of world

A shot where the setting itself moves with dreamlike or choreographed logic (dancing crowds, animated surroundings, rotating/folding world, perpetual spinning) and no dreamer frames it as a dream.

28 Strong destiny

One pole of a flashback circuit: a present-tense figure pausing in reverie (ideally as a dissolve begins), or a visibly 'marked' past image (soft focus, glow, monochrome, slow motion); or an object/space from the past confronted in the present.

29 Weak destiny

A present-tense figure in suspended transit or waiting (road, bus, stranded), or an unanchored, unsignalled fragment of past violence/history that could belong to anyone; no clear optical flashback marker.

30 Forking paths

A shot of doubling or looping: a figure and its double/reflection in the same frame, infinite mirror regress, multiple exposure of one person, a literal forking path, or the looping sea/ship imagery of the Section III scenes.

31 Mark

One visible term of a series: a numbered/listed item, one of several near-identical scenes or objects, or a repeated inscription; strongest if the frame itself shows repetition (rows, lists, sets of three).

32 Demark

A regular series visible in frame with one element out of place (out of step, wrong color, wrong direction, missing, or an object/animal where it cannot belong); the shot must show both the norm and the aberration.

33 Symbol

A single concrete object isolated and charged by framing or light (insert close-up, object in darkness, object as the centre of a composition), strong enough to carry meaning beyond its function.

34a Opsign

Judged on the image alone: a static or near-static shot of a place, object, light-change or crowd with no protagonist pursuing a goal, or an onlooker frozen in pure seeing; whatever moves (water, light, passers-by, animals) just happens and resolves nothing, and the next cut is not motivated by it. Sound is irrelevant to the test; the shot must hold with the sound off.

34b Sonsign

From the image alone (visual search), look for either (a) a listener frozen before an unseen or distant source, head tilted or turned, not moving to answer, or (b) a sound source isolated with no action attached: bells, loudspeakers, radio, phonograph, sirens, whistles, machinery running unattended, rain on water, surf, frogs or birds calling, crowds heard off an empty frame. It can also be a near-empty or dark image where sound would have to carry everything. After that, a listen must confirm three things: the sound stands alone (not music scoring, not dialogue driving plot), it goes on longer than any action it could trigger, and nobody responds to it.

35 Mirrors face to face

A body or place visibly doubled, multiplied or distorted by a mirror, window, water or other reflecting surface inside a single frame; a plain unreflected figure does not count.

36 Limpid and opaque

A frame that holds two states of one image at once: a person between self and role (rehearsal, costume, bare stage), or something visibly half-there (seen through translucent glass, fog, superimposition, or a screen lit from behind).

37 Seed and environment

A shot where a small bounded thing (globe, model, seed, crystal, miniature, tiny painting, screen) visibly contains or foreshadows a larger environment, or a vast built environment that looks like it grew from one.

38 Peaks of the present

A single frame that holds two presents at once: the same person twice, split screen, superimposed people, clocks showing different times, or an ordinary scene with an impossible presence sitting inside it.

39 Sheets of the past

A shot through a space layered with accumulated time (palace galleries, museum halls, archives, storerooms, ruins, old photographs) where different pasts sit side by side, ideally deep focus or long tracking.

40 Powers of the false

A shot that stages testimony, copying or fabrication: people speaking straight to camera in a work setting, forgery or imitation in action, demolition to make way for a replacement, or identical things repeated in series.

41 Bodies of attitude

A body shown in posture rather than purposeful action (sleeping, waiting, yawning, idling, repeating a chore, drifting through a journey), with its tiredness or habits visible in the frame.

42 Bodies of gest

Bodies turned into social ceremony or display in the frame: drill, uniforms, rituals, masks, mime, or official procedure (reading definitions, diagramming plans), with gesture shown as a public, repeated act.

43 Cinema of the brain

An environment presented as a map, model or diagram of thought (chalk or outline plans, overhead grids, mazes, anatomy/neural imagery, cosmic fields), where the place itself is the subject rather than a backdrop for action.

44 Lectosigns

An image that cannot be taken in at a glance and has to be deciphered: abstract light patterns, writing or text filling the frame, disorienting overhead or first-person views, or microscopic and organic forms that look like something else.
