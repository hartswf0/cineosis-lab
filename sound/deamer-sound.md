# Sound in Deamer's cineosis

Source: David Deamer, *Deleuze's Cinema Books* (Edinburgh UP, 2016). Page numbers are the printed page numbers. Quotes are 15 words or fewer. The data is in `deamer-sound.json`.
Labels: **TEXT** means Deamer says it. **INFERENCE** means we are extrapolating.

## The short version

- **Deamer has no sound taxonomy of his own.** Every opsign comes paired with a sonsign ("pure optical and sound situations", pp.138–145). He never gives the sonsign a separate definition, examples or sub-signs. Splitting 34a from 34b is our own move, not his.
- **For sound theory he relies on Michel Chion** (pp.167–168). The discussion sits inside the *lectosign* chapter, as one of five technical coordinates alongside frame, shot, montage and colour (pp.68, 163, 171).
- **Cinema 2 ch.9 barely appears.** The text never mentions the talkie as a concept, speech acts (reflexive, interactive or storytelling), heautonomy, the audio-visual image, sound out-of-field (relative or absolute), free indirect discourse, fabulation, legending, the musical act or the refrain as a concept. "Refrain" appears once, and only as a word for jazz themes (p.184). He cites ch.9 in three places only: C2: 226 and 235, both routed through Chion, and C2: 245 on reading. Any "speech-act" row we build has to come from Deleuze directly, not from Deamer.

## What Deamer actually says (TEXT)

1. **Sound is image from the start.** Sound waves on the eardrum make an "auditory image" (p.6). Both regimes are built from "actual visual and sound images of matter in space as movement" (p.12). A solid perceiver is built by seeing *and hearing* with a character (p.13).
2. **The movement-image binds sound to sight.** The movement-image flows from one actual image to the next, and that flow includes "the linkage of visual and sound image" (p.143). With the talkie, music moved out of the pit and speech moved off the intertitles onto the track, which strengthened realism. Sound became "a new dimension of the visual image" (C2: 226, p.167).
3. **Chion's schemas.**
   - First schema: on-screen, off-screen (acousmatic) and nondiegetic sound. Sounds can move between these categories.
   - Second schema: ambient ("territory-sound"), internal (objective breathing or subjective voiced thought), and on-the-air (radio, phone) (p.167).
4. **Synchresis and the contract.** Chion says there is no separate soundtrack. Sound and image relate *vertically*: synchresis welds a sound to a visible event, the way a voice sticks to a face. For Deleuze this makes sound a "fourth dimension of the visual image", one continuum (C2: 235, p.168). The relation is a contract, not a natural harmony.
5. **How the time-image breaks the contract** (p.168). The tactics come from Chion:
   - suppress music that props up affect or action;
   - remove sound effects, which shrinks the spatial out-of-field;
   - denaturalise the voice, either as theatrical speech, as textual speech (the voice overloads the image), by decentering (the image overloads the voice), or with foreign language and no subtitles.

   These tactics "expose the audiovisual contract and create the time-image".
6. **Sonsigns.** A sonsign is the actual sound image cut off from motor extension. It is the zeroness of the time-image (pp.138–144) and is immediately a hyalosign, one of the "visual and sonic fragments" (p.145). An any-space-whatever can host "a purely optical or sound situation" (C2: 5, p.60) and so become an opsign-sonsign (pp.86, 197–198). The lectosign forces us to read the sound image as well as the optical one (C2: 24, p.45).
7. **Out-of-field** (p.164). Deamer discusses this in *visual* framing terms only. One out-of-field is closed and continuous. The other subsists within the frame and opens onto the Open and the virtual. Carrying this over to sound is **INFERENCE**. The only textual bridge is p.168's link between sound effects and the spatial out-of-field.
8. **Two Section II movement-image links:**
   - Musicals and dance films are cases of *movement of world* (p.125).
   - "Reverb-sound" marks the *strong destiny* flashback (p.128).
